# M4 readability evaluation: our output vs the predecessor's shipped text

**This is an evaluation, not a gate.** Nothing here is asserted by a test and
nothing here blocks the milestone. Its job is to say honestly how readable the
M4 decompiler's output is next to the artifact that actually shipped, and to
name the places where ours is *worse* as precisely as the places where it is
better. A report that only lists wins is not an evaluation.

**Commit.** All figures measured 2026-08-12 against commit
`3ae72c6e43c27188e5a63372cd36a0cb594a07b4` (branch `phase4-m4-loops`). Our side
is `build/decompiled/*.lua`, regenerated by `scripts/run_corpus.py` at that
commit. Everything committed after it — the task-12 tree and the whole-branch
review fixes — is documentation, comments and docstrings, plus four
behaviour-preserving edits under `src/` (re-derived from
`git diff 3ae72c6..HEAD -- src/`, which touches ten files in total — the
other five are comment/docstring-only): `emit/__init__.py` added
`print_chunk_or_raise` to its imports and `__all__` (a package-surface change,
not a behaviour one — the function already existed and was already called via
`pyluadec.emit.lua`); `sir/build.py` extracted a verbatim-duplicated
recognizer cascade into `_match_loop`; `sir/regions.py` removed the `Fallback`
dataclass (dropped from the `Region` union) because nothing ever constructed
one — `build_sir` raises `StructureError` instead and the caller decides —
with `sir/__init__.py`'s imports and `__all__` updated to match, the same
class of package-surface change as the `emit/__init__.py` one; and
`structure/structured.py` changed an `assert certified is not None` to an
explicit `raise RuntimeError(...)` on the same condition, since `python -O`
strips asserts and this check sits between a certification verdict and what
gets written to `build/decompiled/`. Re-running `scripts/run_corpus.py`
afterwards reproduces `build/decompiled/` and `corpus/ledger.jsonl`
byte-for-byte, which `tests/test_ledger_ratchet.py` asserts.

**The comparison target.** The predecessor's shipped text output,
`<predecessor>/post_process/input/*.lua` — 229 UTF-8 files, all present
(`/root/pyluadec` in the original environment; measured at
`/Users/fotile96/pyluadec` here). That artifact is the *localization input* for
《セカンドノベル》: it was read, edited and recompiled by humans, and it
contains no `TODO`/`WARN` markers anywhere (verified: 0 of 229 files). It is a
demanding baseline precisely because it was good enough to ship.

**Line counts** throughout are `bytes.splitlines()` counts, so a file without a
trailing newline is counted the same as one with. `wc -l` differs by one on
such files (e.g. the predecessor's `snr_common.lua`: 5113 here, 5112 by
`wc -l`).

## 1. Quantitative

### 1.1 How much of the corpus ships structured

| measure | value |
| --- | --- |
| files shipping structured source | **212 / 229** (92 at M3 close) |
| files still shipping the M2 fallback | **17 / 229** |
| unique proto shapes certifying `L3` | **1389 / 1478 = 94.0%** |
| proto occurrences certifying `L3` | **1855 / 1944 = 95.4%** |

**Counting files overstates the coverage, and the honest denominator is
lines.** The 17 fallback files are the *large* ones: they account for
**27623 of 57998 predecessor lines — 47.6%** of the corpus's shipped text. So
by file count we structure 93% of the corpus; by volume of the text a human
would actually read, we structure **52.4%**.

The 17: `config.lua`, `snr_common.lua`, `snr_e1_10_s.lua`,
`snr_fragment_main.lua`, `snr_graphic.lua`, `snr_lib.lua`, `snr_mainmenu.lua`,
`snr_memory.lua`, `snr_music.lua`, `snr_novel.lua`, `snr_s1_10_s.lua` …
`snr_s6_10_s.lua`, `snr_story_main.lua`.

**A striking consequence of the per-chunk landing rule.** Certification is
per proto; the *artifact* choice is per chunk, and a chunk ships structured
only if its root certifies. Measured: of the **781** proto occurrences living
inside those 17 files, **692 (88.6%) individually certify `L3`** — and none of
that reaches the shipped file. Equivalently: all **89** non-`L3` proto
occurrences in the entire corpus (1944 − 1855) are inside those 17 files, and
they take 692 certified siblings and ancestors down with them, because
`print_chunk` inlines the whole tree at each `CLOSURE` site. This is the
largest single readability lever available and it is not a new algorithm — it
is the 89.

### 1.2 Line-count ratio, ours vs predecessor, by population

Reported separately because the two populations are not comparable, exactly as
expected.

| population | n | our lines | predecessor lines | aggregate ratio | median | min | max | within 0.5×–2.0× |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shipping **structured** | 212 | 29898 | 30375 | **0.98×** | 0.95× | 0.78× | 1.03× | **212 / 212** |
| shipping **fallback** | 17 | 120246 | 27623 | **4.35×** | 3.85× | 2.43× | 5.01× | **0 / 17** |

Structured output is not merely the same order of magnitude as the shipped
artifact — it is **marginally shorter in aggregate**, and every one of the 212
files lands inside 0.78×–1.03×. That is a stronger result than "same order of
magnitude", and an earlier draft of this section attached an unmeasured cause
to it (indentation, and predecessor-side `local`-hoisting) — indentation has
no line-count effect by construction, and the `local` claim is contradicted by
§1.4's own row: the predecessor emits **906** line-leading `local`s against
our **1329**, i.e. hoisting makes the predecessor *shorter* in `local` lines,
not longer. Re-measured properly, by summing whole categories of
line-leading tokens over the same 212 files:

| category | ours | predecessor | delta (ours − predecessor) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| control/decl keywords (`if`/`elseif`/`else`/`end`/`while`/`for`/`local`/`return`/`break`/`repeat`/`until`/`do`/`function`) | 5610 | 4774 | **+836** |
| lines whose first token is a bare synthesized variable (`l<d>_<d>` for us, `v<N>` for the predecessor) | 1214 | 2522 | **−1308** |
| every other line (calls, string literals, table bodies, …) | 23074 | 23079 | **−5** |

`836 − 1308 − 5 = −477`, exactly the measured aggregate delta — this fully
accounts for the gap; nothing is left over. Both rows re-measured
independently 2026-08-12 and reproduce to the line.

**The two rows are not independent, and the largest single thing they show is
a re-categorization, not a saving.** The keyword row's `+836` decomposes
exactly as `+208 if`, `+205 else`, `−208 elseif`, `+208 end` — the
missing-`elseif` defect of §1.4, netting `+413` — plus **`+423 local`**. That
`local` row is the same lines the var row is losing under a different label:
the predecessor writes `v2 = 0`, which leads with a variable; we write
`local l1_0 = 0`, which leads with a keyword. `snr_crd_10.lua` at the same
point in both files (ours 52–55, the predecessor's 56–59) is the shape
verbatim:

```lua
-- ours                    -- predecessor
local l1_0 = 0             v2 = 0
local l1_1 = 0             v3 = 0
local l1_2 = (-1)          v4 = -1
while l1_0 < 1 do          while v2 < 1 do
```

So of the var row's `−1308`, `423` are lines we still emit and merely count in
the other row; the remaining **`−885`** are lines the predecessor emits that we
do not emit at all. Corpus-wide, of the 1768 `v<N> = …` lines in these 212
files, 739 are function definitions (a wash — both sides emit one line per
closure) and **358 of the remaining 1029 are reused within the next 3 lines**,
consistent with a one-shot temporary that our reconstructor's expression
inlining does not materialize as its own line. That is a line-accounting
result, not a read of the predecessor's emitter source — *why* it favors
splitting these into separate assignments is not established here. We are
*longer* wherever the predecessor used `elseif` (see §2.2).

**Two mechanisms an earlier draft of this section named are false, and are
recorded here so they are not re-derived.** (i) "a loop-guard temporary we
fold into `while true do`": there are **0** occurrences of `while true` across
all 212 structured files, and `snr_crd_10.lua` emits the counter as
`local l1_0 = 0` … `while l1_0 < 1 do`, the same shape as the predecessor's.
(ii) "a condition value we inline directly into the `if`": `snr_crd_10.lua`
emits `local l1_3 = oCard["selectCardObj"]()` on its own line and only then
`if l1_3 == 9 then`. The two pairs are not alike: the counter/`while` pair
does have two lines intervening in every cited file, on both sides — but the
condition-value pair does not. The assignment is immediately followed by the
`if`, zero lines intervening, in all three cited files, both sides (ours:
`snr_crd_10.lua:56-57`, `snr_crd_100.lua:52-53`, `snr_crd_103.lua:217-218`;
predecessor's: `60-61`, `56-57`, `223-224`). An earlier draft of this section
claimed neither pair was immediately followed by anything; that claim was
itself unmeasured and is corrected here rather than re-derived. The
arithmetic above is the one that was checked against the emitted files
rather than inferred from them.

The fallback population is 4.35× the predecessor and could not be otherwise:
it is a `__pld_pc` dispatch state machine, one basic block per `if` arm.

## 2. Qualitative: three files side by side

The brief asked for a clean scenario file, a heavy-logic file "such as
`snr_common.lua`", and a still-fallback file. **`snr_common.lua` is one of the
17 that still ship fallback**, so it serves as the third exemplar rather than
the second, and a structured heavy-logic file (`snr_s4_10_fa.lua`, a
1183-line state-machine driver) takes the second slot. The substitution is
noted rather than silently made.

### 2.1 Clean scenario file — `snr_crd_10.lua` (structured; ours 82 lines, predecessor 86)

Ours:

```lua
local l0_0 = function()
cPlayBGMWrap(cGetFileName("bgm", "bgm_5_x"))
comPoseLoad("po2_aya_f_1_a")
vms(283, "NONVOICE", "\227\128\144\231\155\180\229\147\137\227\128\145\227\128\140…")
…
comPoseDelete()
end
…
if not (gameSave["myCard"][gameSave["fragmentDoCardId"]]["hold"] == 1) then
  gameSave["myCard"][126]["hold"] = 1
  comAyanoTimeRefresh()
end
```

Predecessor:

```lua
local v0, v1, v2, v3, v4, v5
v0 = function()
    local v0
    cPlayBGMWrap(cGetFileName("bgm", "bgm_5_x"))
    comPoseLoad("po2_aya_f_1_a")
    vms(283, "NONVOICE", "【直哉】「セクション１，というのが、…」")
    …
    comPoseDelete()
end
…
    if not (gameSave.myCard[gameSave.fragmentDoCardId].hold == 1) then
        gameSave.myCard[126].hold = 1
        comAyanoTimeRefresh()
    end
```

**Assessment.** On control flow the two are indistinguishable — same
statements, same order, same `if not (…) then`. Ours is *better* on scoping:
each closure is declared where it is defined (`local l0_0 = function()`), where
the predecessor hoists a file-wide `local v0, v1, v2, v3, v4, v5` block and
then assigns into it, and additionally emits a dead `local v0` at the top of
every function body. Ours is *worse* in three visible ways, and for this
corpus the first is severe. **Every non-ASCII string constant prints as decimal
escapes.** This is a Japanese visual novel whose shipped artifact exists to be
translated; `"\227\128\144\231\155\180…"` where the predecessor prints
`"【直哉】「…」"` is the single largest readability gap in the whole
comparison, and it is a direct consequence of the standing rule that
bytecode-derived strings stay `bytes` and are never decoded. Second, we print
`gameSave["myCard"][126]["hold"]` where the predecessor prints
`gameSave.myCard[126].hold`; the dot form is a pure emitter change (a
valid-identifier-and-not-a-keyword test that `emit/lua.py::_ident` already
implements for the opposite direction). Third, function bodies are not
indented — the body of `l0_0` sits at column 0 while the `if` inside it indents
normally, which reads as though the function had no block at all. That last one
may be *deliberate* downstream coupling (CLAUDE.md records that
`post_process/`'s line-anchored regexes expect 顶层无缩进), so it should not be
changed without deciding that question.


**Assessment.** The `while` loop, its condition, and the dispatch are recovered
correctly — M4's central result, and this is a file M3 could not structure at
all. Ours is also *better* on locals: `local l1_0 = 0` is declared inside
`main` where the predecessor hoists it, and we do not emit the predecessor's
dead `local v18`. But the missing `elseif` turns a flat 17-way dispatch into a
17-deep staircase, and this is where our output reads decisively worse than the
shipped artifact. Measured across the 212 structured files, maximum nesting

| file | ours | predecessor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `snr_s7_10_f.lua` | **61** | 5 |

| `snr_s8_10_f.lua` | 18 | 4 |

Sixty-one levels of indentation in `snr_s7_10_f.lua` against the predecessor's
five. It affects 16 of the 212 files; in the other 196 ours is actually
*shallower* than the predecessor (median depth 2 vs 3), because we do not
indent function bodies. Two opposite defects, and they do not cancel — they
just make the median look fine while the worst case is unreadable. Collapsing
`else` + a single `if` into `elseif` is a local printer change, it cannot
change what recompiles (Lua parses the two identically), and it would close the
`if`/`else`/`end` count gaps in §1.4 in one move. It is the highest-value
readability fix in this report.

### 2.3 Still-fallback file — `snr_common.lua` (ours 19694 lines, predecessor 5113; 3.85×)

Predecessor:

```lua
local v0, v1, v2, i0, v3, v4, v5, i4, …
debagFlag = false
gameSave = {}
gameSave.startMenuState = 0
gameSave.sceneid = ""
gameSave.myCard = {}
gameSave.tutorial = {}
gameSave.tutorial.firstStory = 0
```

Ours:

```lua
local __pld_select, __pld_unpack, __pld_tonumber, __pld_error = select, unpack, tonumber, error
local __pld_get = function(R, B, i) local x = R[i] if B[i] then return x.v end return x end
local __pld_F = {}
__pld_F["main_0"] = function(__pld_U)
return function(__pld_p0)

__pld_R[1] = string
__pld_R[1] = __pld_R[1]["gsub"]
__pld_R[2] = __pld_R[0]
__pld_R[3] = "0"
```

**Assessment.** There is no way to describe this as competitive. The
predecessor's `snr_common.lua` is ordinary, editable Lua; ours is a register
machine transcribed into Lua text, and for the largest and most important file
in the corpus (124 KB of bytecode, 147 protos) that is a straight loss. The
scaffolding is honest — every `__pld_R`/`__pld_B` construct exists because the
fallback is semantically exact and refuses to guess — and the file *does*
compile and *does* reproduce the original's observable behaviour under both
probes. But nobody would translate a script from it.

What makes this the most actionable entry in the report rather than the most
discouraging one: **104 of `snr_common.lua`'s 147 protos individually certify
`L3` right now.** The structured reconstruction of most of this file already
exists and passes the gate. It does not ship because the chunk root does not
certify and `print_chunk` inlines the whole tree, so one refusal anywhere in
the ancestor chain forfeits everything above it. The same shape holds for
`snr_lib.lua` (ours 350 lines vs the predecessor's 93, 3.76×): 1 of its 3
protos certifies, and the root's refusal — `structure: pc 73: if targets out of
range`, on a root whose own body is 5 instructions — is a *descendant's*
failure reported without saying so (M5 agenda item 8).

### 2.4 Names: `l0_0` vs `v0` — a wash, not a loss

The brief anticipated that our synthesized `l0_0` would read worse than the
predecessor's `v0`. Having looked at both at length, that is not what the
comparison shows. `v0` is shorter, which helps in dense expressions. `l0_0`
encodes closure depth as well as register, which disambiguates the extremely
common case of nested closures reusing the same register numbers — the
predecessor handles that by shadowing (`v0` inside `v0`, visible in §2.1),
which is strictly more confusing. Neither carries meaning; both are the
consequence of a stripped corpus. Treat name semanticisation as low-value work
on the strength of this comparison, not as a debt this milestone incurred.

## 3. Summary of where each side wins

**Ours reads better:**

- Real, verified block structure on 212 of 229 files — and the `while`/`break`
  counts match the predecessor's *exactly* on those files (190/190, 175/175).
- Locals are declared at their real scope, not hoisted into a file-wide
  `local v0, v1, …` block, and we emit no dead declarations.
- Zero `__pld_*` scaffolding in any structured file — the artifact is either
  clean Lua or visibly a fallback, never a mixture.
- Every shipped structured file is *certified*: it recompiled through the real
  `luac_sz4` and matched the original field by field. The predecessor's output
  was validated by human review and in-engine play, which is a different and
  weaker kind of evidence about any individual line.

(Median nesting depth is 2 for us and 3 for the predecessor, but that is
*not* a win — it is the un-indented function bodies listed below showing up
as a smaller number. It is recorded so nobody quotes it as one.)

**Ours reads worse:**

- **Non-ASCII strings print as decimal escapes.** For this corpus, the single
  biggest gap.
- **No `elseif`.** 208 of them on the 212-file population become `else` + nested
  `if` + `end`; worst case 61 levels of nesting against the predecessor's 5.
- **No dot sugar** for string keys: `t["k"]` where the predecessor writes `t.k`.
- **Function bodies are not indented**, so a function's body is visually flush
  with its declaration (possibly deliberate — downstream coupling).
- Unary minus prints as `(-1)`, not `-1`.
- **17 files still ship a register-machine fallback**, and they are 47.6% of
  the corpus's text by volume. The predecessor structured all 229.

Every item on the "worse" list except the last is a local change in
`emit/lua.py` that cannot affect what recompiles. The last one is M5 agenda
item 3, and its leverage is concentrated: 89 uncertified proto occurrences are
holding 692 certified ones hostage.

## Appendix: rollout after hybrid emission (M5 task 6, 2026-08-12)

The last item on the "worse" list above — "17 files still ship a
register-machine fallback ... 47.6% of the corpus's text by volume" — is the
one this appendix updates. The 47.6% figure itself did not reproduce on any
basis when it was re-measured (see `docs/runbooks/structured-pipeline.md`,
"A brief number that did not reproduce"); the closest comparable basis is
bytecode instruction volume, and all three columns below are measured on the
committed `corpus/ledger.jsonl` and `build/decompiled/`.

Hybrid emission (`structure.structured.certify_hybrid`) replaced the
chunk-level all-or-nothing landing rule: a chunk whose root does not certify
now ships structured code with the fallback state machine substituted only at
the CLOSURE sites that could not be certified, provided the assembled chunk
still compares equal to the original field for field everywhere outside those
subtrees.

| measure | M4 close | M5 task 5 | **M5 task 6** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| files shipping some structured source | 212 / 229 | 216 / 229 | **222 / 229** (216 fully + 6 hybrid) |
| files shipping a whole-file state machine | 17 | 13 | **7** |
| proto occurrences whose text is structured | 1163 / 1944 (59.8%) | 1379 / 1944 (70.9%) | **1747 / 1944 (89.9%)** |
| bytecode instruction volume shipping as a state machine | 46.1% | 34.4% | **14.2%** |
| decompiled output lines inside whole-file-fallback files | 80.1% | — | **37.0%** (31884/86228) |

Two cells are derived rather than re-measured, and are marked here rather than
presented as fresh measurements: the M4-close occurrence count is
`1944 − 781`, using the 781 this report measures above, and the M4-close
instruction volume is the 46.1% from the runbook's re-measurement of the same
17 files. The M5 task 5 column was measured this session from the ledger as it
stood at `31e1174`; the M5 task 6 column from the ledger and
`build/decompiled/` as they stand after this task.

Per hybrid file, the fraction of its own instructions that ship structured:
`snr_s5_10_s.lua` 95.1%, `snr_s1_10_s.lua` 93.3%, `snr_s3_10_s.lua` 89.2%,
`snr_s4_10_s.lua` 88.6%, `snr_fragment_main.lua` 45.2%,
`snr_story_main.lua` 13.2%.

**The 7 that remain are a different problem from the 17 were.** The M4
analysis was right that "89 uncertified proto occurrences are holding 692
certified ones hostage" — hybrid emission is exactly the release, and it moved
6 files. It did not move the other 7 because in each of them the failing proto
is the chunk's own **root**: `snr_graphic.lua`'s main body does not print at
all, and `config.lua`, `snr_mainmenu.lua`, `snr_memory.lua`, `snr_music.lua`,
`snr_novel.lua` and `snr_common.lua` print a main chunk that recompiles to a
different proto. A hybrid can substitute a child; there is nothing to
substitute a root with, and shipping an uncertified root is exactly what the
landing gate exists to prevent. Their leverage is now in the reconstructor,
not in the landing rule.
