# M5 quality evaluation

**This is an evaluation, not a gate**, in the same sense as
`docs/reports/m4-readability.md`. The gates are `tests/test_m5_exit.py` (four
floors), `tests/test_ledger_ratchet.py` (the ledger is a fixed point of its own
generator) and `scripts/ratchet_job.sh` (baseline diff, downgrade = fail). This
file's job is to say what M5 actually bought, in the predecessor's own units
where possible, and to name the places our output is still worse as precisely
as the places it is better.

**Provenance.** Every figure below was measured on 2026-08-13 at the M5 task 12
tree, on macOS arm64, with
`PYLUADEC_CORPUS_DIR=/Users/fotile96/pyluadec/others/script_to_decompile` and
`build/decompiled/` regenerated by `scripts/run_corpus.py` at that commit.
Figures carried in from earlier tasks are labelled as such. Where a figure
carried forward did **not** reproduce, the discrepancy is stated rather than
smoothed over — §5 collects those.

**The comparison target**, unchanged from M4: the predecessor's shipped
localization input, `/Users/fotile96/pyluadec/post_process/input/*.lua`, 229
UTF-8 files. It was read, edited and recompiled by humans for the shipped
Chinese localization of 《セカンドノベル》, which is what makes it a demanding
baseline. Line counts throughout are `bytes.splitlines()` counts.

## 1. The ported metric

`pyluadec.verify.semantic_score` (M5 task 3) is a line-for-line port of the
predecessor's `semantic_eval.py`: nine weighted components over per-proto
signatures, scored between the original bytecode and a real `luac_sz4`
recompile of our emitted source. `scripts/quality_score.py` is the CLI;
`tests/test_m5_exit.py::test_m5_exit_quality_no_regression` is the committed
arm, and the two agree to six decimals.

### 1.1 Corpus average across the milestone

| point | avg_score | structured | hybrid | whole-file fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M5 start (task 3, first measurement) | 0.935633 | 212 | — | 17 |
| after task 4 (structure-refusal leaves) | 0.946390 | 215 | — | 14 |
| after task 5 (mismatch/reconstruct leaves) | 0.950580 | 216 | — | 13 |
| after task 6 (hybrid emission) | **0.968833** | 216 | 6 | **7** |
| **at task 12 (this measurement)** | **0.968833** | **216** | **6** | **7** |

Tasks 7–12 changed the printer's surface, the ratchet, the L2 gate and the
documents; none of them was supposed to move the metric and none of them did.

**Against the predecessor's stated baseline of 0.997: we are below it, and the
gap is diagnosed rather than explained away.** The arithmetic is why M5's exit
was re-scoped (spec §2 decision table, 2026-08-12): with 216 files at *exactly*
1.000000, the remaining 13 would have to average ≥ 0.947, and converting all 7
remaining whole-file fallbacks to hybrids at the measured hybrid quality of
0.816791 reaches only 0.9896. 0.997 needs essentially every file at ~1.0 —
i.e. the reconstruction residual closed. That is M6's exit now.

### 1.2 The distribution, and the honest note

| population | n | mean | min | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fully structured | 216 | **1.000000** | 1.000000 | 1.000000 |
| hybrid | 6 | 0.816791 | 0.456696 | 0.971391 |
| whole-file fallback | 7 | 0.137429 | 0.075326 | 0.180702 |
| **all** | **229** | **0.968833** | 0.075326 | 1.000000 |

**Every file below 0.997 is one of the 13 that do not ship fully structured
source, and there are no others.** So at file granularity the metric is still a
monotone function of the certification outcome — it discovers no defect that
certification missed. That is a real (if negative) result about the instrument
and it should be stated plainly rather than presented as a clean bill of
health: the metric earns its keep by *grading inside* the failing 13, not by
finding anything among the 216.

The 216 scoring exactly 1.000000 is expected by construction and is **not a
self-comparison**: `quality_score.py` scores `load_file(f)` against
`load_bytes(oracle.compile_source(res.source))`, i.e. the original bytecode
against a real recompile of our emitted text. `L3` already asserts field-for-
field proto equality modulo lineinfo, and the metric's signature is a strict
coarsening of that comparison, so a certified chunk cannot score below 1.0.

## 2. Structured coverage by volume

Counting files flatters us, which is why M4's readability report introduced a
volume basis. **M4's figure — "the 17 fallback files are 27623 of 57998
predecessor lines, 47.6%" — reproduces exactly on its own stated basis**
(re-measured here: 27623/57998 = 47.6%, on M4's 17-file set). M5 task 3
reported that it "did not reproduce under any of five bases"; that is true of
the five bases task 3 chose and does not contradict M4, because the
predecessor-line basis was not among them. Both statements were right about
different denominators. The lesson is the one this project keeps relearning:
quote the denominator with the number.

Re-measured post-hybrid, across five bases:

| basis | total | 7 whole-file fallbacks | 6 hybrids | the 13 together | the 216 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| predecessor lines | 57998 | 8888 (15.3%) | 12671 (21.8%) | 37.2% | **62.8%** |
| our output lines | 85588 | 31884 (37.3%) | 18281 (21.4%) | 58.6% | 41.4% |
| bytecode bytes | 3024579 | 181527 (6.0%) | 707524 (23.4%) | 29.4% | 70.6% |
| bytecode instructions | 215218 | 24243 (11.3%) | 49719 (23.1%) | 34.4% | 65.6% |
| proto occurrences | 1944 | 189 (9.7%) | 376 (19.3%) | 29.1% | 70.9% |

The bases disagree by a factor of six on the fallback column, so **no single
one of them is "the" coverage number**. What holds across all five is the
direction: the not-fully-structured files are 29–59% of the corpus by volume
while being 5.7% of it by file count.

The sharper number, and the one that does not need a choice of basis, is
per-occurrence: of the 1944 proto occurrences, **1747 (89.9%) ship text from
the structured printer** and 197 (10.1%) ship the `__pld_pc` state machine.
Weighted by instruction count the same split is **85.8% / 14.2%** (M5 start:
65.6% / 34.4%). That is the M5 headline stated without a flattering
denominator.

## 3. Readability, re-measured after task 7

M4 named five printer-level places where our output read *worse* than the
predecessor's, plus a sixth structural one. Task 7 closed all five (output
surface v2). Re-measured here over the **216** fully-structured files (M4's
population was 212), predecessor side over the same 216:

| axis | M4 | now | predecessor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| max nesting depth (worst file) | **61** (`snr_s7_10_f`) | **8** (`snr_s2_10_s`) | **10** (`snr_s2_10_s`) |
| files where ours is deeper than the predecessor | 16 of 212 | **0 of 216** | — |
| line-leading `elseif` | 0 | **282** | 278 |
| `["` (bracketed string index) | 6085 | **0** | 0 |
| high-byte decimal escapes | 991233 | **0** | 0 |
| `(-` (parenthesised unary minus) | 570 | **0** | 0 |
| line-leading `while` | 190 | **196** | **196** |
| line-leading `break` | 175 | **178** | **178** |
| aggregate line ratio (ours ÷ predecessor) | 0.98× | **0.972×** | — |
| `__pld_*` occurrences | 0 | **0** | 0 |

`while` and `break` still match the predecessor **exactly**, now on a larger
population. Nesting depth is the reversal: the axis M4 called our worst is now
one where we are shallower than the shipped artifact in 3 files and never
deeper in any. Per-file line ratios span 0.778×–0.992× with a median of 0.947×.

Non-ASCII bytes: **991233 in our 216 files against 991263 in the
predecessor's** — a 30-byte difference on 991k, i.e. the Japanese script text
now survives to the artifact essentially verbatim. The 991233 is exactly the
count of high-byte decimal escapes task 7 removed, which is the arithmetic
closing.

### 3.1 UTF-8, side by side

`snr_crd_10.lua` line 4, against the predecessor's line 6:

```lua
-- ours (M5)
vms(283, "NONVOICE", "【直哉】「セクション１，というのが、どうやらこの物語の区切りを意味するみたいだね」")
-- predecessor
vms(283, "NONVOICE", "【直哉】「セクション１，というのが、どうやらこの物語の区切りを意味するみたいだね」")
```

Byte-identical. At M4 our side of that line was
`"\227\128\144\231\155\180\229\147\137\227\128\145…"` — 128 escape sequences
for 43 characters, on a corpus whose entire downstream purpose is translation.

**Say what the evidence for the escape path is.** Measured 2026-08-12 and
unchanged: the corpus holds 49063 string constants, 17411 of them non-ASCII and
**0 of them non-UTF-8**. So the fallback-to-escapes branch of
`emit/lua.py::_str_lit` is exercised only by synthetic fixtures
(`tests/test_emit_readability.py`'s `_LONE_CONT` / `_TRUNCATED` and the
reviewer's 34 hostile byte sequences). A green corpus says nothing about it.

### 3.3 Where we still read differently — and it is not obviously worse

The one systematic difference left on the 216 is name and scope style, and it
is a wash rather than a loss (M4 §2.4 reached the same conclusion for different
reasons). File heads, `snr_s4_10_fa.lua`:

```lua
-- ours                                   -- predecessor
local l0_0 = function(l1_0, l1_1)         local v0, v1, v2, ..., v17
    local l1_2 = function()               v0 = function(p0, p1)
    end                                       local v0, v1, v2
    comSectionNonLabelBegin(l1_0, l1_1)       v0 = function()
                                              end
                                              comSectionNonLabelBegin(p0, p1)
```

Ours declares each closure where it is defined; the predecessor hoists a
file-wide `local v0, …, v17` block and then assigns into it, and emits a dead
`local v0, v1, v2` at the top of every function body. Neither side recovers
real names — the corpus is stripped, `locvars` is empty in all 1944 protos, so
both synthesize. The line-leading `local` counts differ for exactly this
reason (ours 1619, predecessor 1020) and it is a re-categorisation, not extra
text: `local l1_0 = 0` leads with a keyword where `v2 = 0` leads with a
variable.

## 4. L2 reach — the measured outcome, which is one file

`corpus/ledger.jsonl` carries the `l2` field per occurrence, with a row-level
pure aggregate (`verify.ledger.row_l2`, granted iff every occurrence is, at the
minimum coverage). Measured on the committed ledger:

| | value |
| --- | --- |
| files with an authored key sequence | 5 (13 sequences) |
| files **granted** L2 | **1** — `snr_lib.lua`, `L2@100%` |
| files refused | 4 — `snr_common`, `snr_graphic`, `snr_memory`, `snr_music`, all `taint-static` |
| ledger rows carrying the badge | **2** of 1478 |
| occurrences carrying `{"cov": 1.0}` | **3** of 1944 |
| occurrences carrying `{"refused": "taint-static"}` | **164** |
| files with no `pressButton` at all | 220 of 229 |

`snr_lib.lua`'s badge covers 3 of its 3 protos; the block-level diagnostic
interval (CLI and runbook only, never the ledger) is `[0.35, 0.91]` of 57
blocks, 32 of which share an entry line.

**The exit checklist line "L2 badge on ≥ the two checkpoint files" is not
satisfied, and cannot be without weakening the gate.** Both historical
key-sequence checkpoints live in `snr_common.lua` (so "the two checkpoint
files" is one file), and `snr_common.lua` is refused `taint-static` with 236
`blind-compare` / 3 `blind-launder` / 50 `blind-loop` / 20 `blind-truthiness`
sites. The refusal is correct: Lua 5.1's `equalobj` compares types before
consulting `__eq`, so a stub proxy compared against a string literal is
*unobservable* to the runtime harness, and `doScriptScene == "<literal>"` is
this corpus's central idiom (re-derived 2026-08-13 on the **original
bytecode** — a `GETGLOBAL "doScriptScene"` whose destination is an `EQ` operand
within three instructions: **427 sites across 23 files**, out of **879** total
`GETGLOBAL "doScriptScene"` reads in those same 23 files — so 452 reads are not
comparison sites. The 879 is the **total**, not an additional population; an
earlier revision of this paragraph wrote "plus 879 bare reads", double-counting
the 427. The noun matters twice over: an earlier count of 310 sites across 20
files was taken from the decompiled artifact TEXT rather than the bytecode and
undercounted prevalence by 27%, because three of the 23 ship fallback and never
spell the name). Four rounds of tightening in task 9 found thirty granting
routes and never once moved the reach off 1 of 5 — every fix refused a fixture,
none refused a corpus file that should have been granted.

What *is* green is the pair of checkpoints themselves:
`tests/test_keyseq_checkpoints.py` drives `snr_common.lua` under scripted key
sequences and asserts the two historical bug properties (the `OptionMain`
double clamp, the `ConfigMain` CIRCLE gate/wait) **on the original's trace as
well as ours**, with **10** parametrised bug injections proving the assertions
fire (re-counted 2026-08-13; task 10's report said seven, its round-0 figure).
The both-sides guarantee comes from the property functions being applied to
each trace separately *after* trace equality is required, not from the
injections: the committed parametrisation mutates **only the artifact side**,
and the both-sides form is an out-of-tree exercise the file discloses. Calling
the injections "both-sides" overstated what is committed. Those tests do not
need the L2 badge and do not claim it; the
badge would have added a coverage measurement, not the property.

## 5. Figures that did not reproduce

Recorded because this project's most expensive recurring defect is prose
asserting a number nobody re-derived.

1. **"the 47.6% figure did not reproduce under any of five bases"** (M5 task 3)
   — it reproduces *exactly* on the basis M4 stated (27623/57998 predecessor
   lines, M4's 17-file set). Task 3 measured five other bases. Both are
   correct; the figure needed its denominator quoted. §2.
2. **"L2 badge on ≥ the two checkpoint files"** (the task 12 brief's exit
   checklist) — both checkpoints are in **one** file, and that file is refused.
   §4.
3. **"suite ~37.7s fixed-ordering"** (carried into task 12) — measured at
   task 12's base commit, fixed ordering, `833 passed in 40.16s`. See the M5
   wrap entry in `CHANGELOG.md` for the post-task figure.
4. **"`-p no:randomly` really does change the figure"** (from M5 task 11,
   repeated in this report's first draft, in `CLAUDE.md` and in the
   `CHANGELOG`) — and **"test ordering makes no measurable difference"**, the
   correction task 12 replaced it with. *Neither was a measurement of
   ordering.* Measured 2026-08-13: no randomiser is installed
   (`pytest --trace-config` registers exactly one third-party plugin,
   `pytest-timeout 2.4.0`; `find_spec("pytest_randomly")` is `None`), and
   `-p no:randomly` deselects a plugin that does not exist, which pytest
   accepts silently (`uv run pytest -p no:randomly --co -q` → 867 collected,
   exit 0). Every A/B in this milestone — task 11's 5 pairs, task 12's 5
   pairs, a reviewer's 10 on a second host — therefore ran both arms in the
   **same collection order**. This is the milestone's signature defect
   surviving into the correction of itself.

   What the runs do support, unchanged: this suite has **1–2s of run-to-run
   variance** (42.25–43.39s and 42.57–43.09s on macOS 15.2 / Apple M4 are two
   samples of one configuration, not two configurations; a reviewer's second
   host gives 39.36–41.13s), so a single-run comparison across sessions cannot
   separate anything from noise. That noise is where this milestone's disputed
   timings came from. Whether ordering matters here is **unmeasured**: it
   needs `pytest-randomly` actually installed, and that is an M6 question.
5. **"15 `CASES` driven by a plain `for` loop"** (M5 agenda item 10) — there
   are **17**; they are now `pytest.mark.parametrize`d, so the count maintains
   itself.
6. **"a chunk ships structured iff its ROOT certifies"** — false since M5
   task 6, and the sweep for it needed three passes. Round 1 of this task
   found two more **live production** sites that a phrase-match had missed
   (`src/pyluadec/pipeline.py`'s module docstring and
   `src/pyluadec/emit/__init__.py`'s), plus two places that drew the retired
   *inference* rather than stating the rule
   (`tests/test_structured_certify.py::test_table_constructor_fold_reorders_statements_but_cannot_ship`
   and its runbook twin). Search for the concept, not the wording.
7. **"`sharing the snapshot OBJECT` changes nothing; only hoisting
   reproduces"** (M5 task 7 review) — re-measured at task 12: *both* variants
   turn `test_while_true_break_guard_recovers_loop` from `L3` to `L1`, and
   there is no third variant, because `open_scope` returns a value and a value
   cannot be shared any other way. The comment in `emit/lua.py` now says what
   was measured.

## 6. What this report does not evaluate

- **Anything about the 5 opcodes the corpus never contains** (`SELF`, `POW`,
  `UNM`, `NOT`, `CLOSE`). Every figure here is corpus-derived, so none of it
  speaks to them; their evidence is the synthetic fixtures named in
  `docs/runbooks/m3-backlog.md`.
- **`repeat`.** 0 `repeat`/`until` lines in all 229 artifacts and 0 `Repeat`
  regions over all 1944 protos (measured 2026-08-13), against 208 `While`
  regions. The recognizer's evidence is entirely synthetic.
- **Runtime behaviour beyond the two standing probes and the one L2 grant.**
  L1.5 and L1.75 are byte-comparison gates over a stubbed environment, not the
  game engine; §4 says what L2 reaches.
- **Readability of the 13.** They are a `__pld_pc` state machine or contain
  one, and there is no way to describe that as competitive — M4 §2.3 said so
  and it is still true of the 7.
